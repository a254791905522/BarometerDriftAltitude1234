Burner charges

Fire a charge and the balloon rises a fixed step. That charge is gone afterward. Later in a run, fewer charges remain, so the moment you spend one matters.

Ballast bags

A ballast bag is the other lift. It also raises the balloon by a fixed amount, and using it permanently consumes that bag. You choose the resource as well as the timing.

Four decisions in the shaft.

You do not steer a free climb. The balloon drifts, the band moves, and each lift is a single spent step. Read the gap, then spend.

  1. 01

    Sight the band

    The target is an altitude band traveling through the shaft. Its place on the next moment is part of the puzzle.

  2. 02

    Pick a lift

    Burner charges and ballast bags are separate supplies. Either one raises the balloon by a fixed amount.

  3. 03

    Spend it once

    The unit you use does not return. A lift spent early is a lift missing when the band shifts again.

  4. 04

    Meet the altitude

    Reach the target altitude while options remain. When both supplies are exhausted, the run has no further raise.

The shaft gets stricter.

As a flight goes on, careful resource management becomes the whole game. The listing describes two pressures that arrive together.

What changes

The goal stays the same: put the balloon on the moving band. The room to miss gets smaller.

  • Supplies thin out Burner charges and ballast bags become more limited. Each remaining unit has to cover a real gap.
  • The band wanders Target movement becomes less predictable. A raise that fit the last position can miss the next one.

On Apple devices
